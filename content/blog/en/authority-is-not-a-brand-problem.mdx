When an AI system recommends a supplier, it is doing something more constrained than forming an opinion. It is assembling a justification from material it can point to.

That distinction matters, because it changes what kind of problem a weak position is.

## Claims and evidence are not the same input

A business describing itself is making a claim. A third party describing that business is providing evidence. Systems weigh the two very differently, and they are right to.

This is why a well-written site can coexist with a weak recommendation position. The site is the claim. It was never the thing being weighed.

## The gap is countable

The useful property of an evidence gap is that it is not mysterious. It is possible to count the independent sources that corroborate one business and compare that number to a competitor's.

Once the gap is a number, it stops being a brand conversation:

1. It has a size, which can be measured
2. It has a composition — which kinds of sources are missing
3. It has a cost, because commissioning evidence has a price
4. It has a completion date, because publication schedules exist

## Why this is better news than it sounds

A brand-preference problem has no defined remedy and no end state. An evidence gap is a supply problem: specific material does not exist, and material can be created.

The work is unglamorous — verification, references, independent coverage, documentation a system can actually cite. But it is bounded, and progress against it can be measured directly rather than inferred from sentiment.
